# Job search strategy for bootcamp AI engineers

Treat the search like a product: weekly metrics, a funnel, and iteration. Random easy-apply spam is how people burn out.

## Weekly operating cadence

- **5–8 tailored applications**, not 40 generic ones. Tailor the top third of the resume and the first paragraph of the note to their stack (RAG, evals, Python APIs).
- **5 outbound conversations**: alumni, meetup speakers, engineers who wrote the blog post you used.
- **1 public artifact**: blog, demo video, or GitHub README improvement on your main RAG project.
- **Interview practice**: 3 coding problems and one mock system-design (even 25 minutes with a peer).

Track: applications, replies, screens, onsites, offers. If replies are under ~5% after 30 tailored apps, the resume/projects are the bottleneck, not "the market" alone.

## Where to apply

Prioritize teams that already ship LLM features: applied AI, search, support automation, developer tools, internal knowledge assistants. Avoid roles that require a publication record unless you have one.

Good signals on a job post:

- Python + APIs + retrieval/evals in the description
- "Own the quality of answers" or "production LLM"
- Seniority that matches your shipped work (often IC2/IC3)

Bad signals for a first role:

- "Must have trained large models from scratch"
- Unpaid trial projects that look like real work
- Title is AI Engineer but the description is pure sales demo wrangling with no engineering

## Outreach that gets answers

Short note:

1. One sentence on who you are.
2. One sentence on a project that matches their product.
3. One specific question or offer (review their docs, share a 60-second demo).

Attach or link a README with a metric. Do not send a five-paragraph life story.

## Offers and leveling

Compare: scope of the work, mentor quality, production ownership, and cash + equity. A title inflation ("Staff AI Engineer" at a two-person startup) matters less than whether you will debug retrieval in production.

If you have no leverage yet, optimize for **learning velocity**: a team with evals, code review, and real users.

## Mindset

Rejection is mostly volume and fit. Update the resume from every screen: if three interviewers asked about evals, put evals higher. Keep shipping the same two projects until they are embarrassingly well documented.
